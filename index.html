Learn the skills

Weekend tutorials from the crew

Free step-by-step guides. No gatekeeping — if we know it, you know it.

Stain a fence like a pro
  1. Pressure-wash the fence and let it dry 48 hours. Stain on damp wood peels by fall.
  2. Tape off anything you don't want stained and lay drop cloths along the base.
  3. Stir — never shake — the stain. Brush it on with the grain, one board at a time.
  4. Back-brush every section within 10 minutes so it soaks in even.
  5. Two thin coats beats one thick coat. Let the first dry fully before the second.
Patch drywall in a weekend
  1. Cut the damaged area into a clean square and screw in a patch panel.
  2. Tape the seams with mesh tape — paper tape bubbles on beginners.
  3. First mud coat: thin, wide, feathered 6 inches past the tape. Let it dry overnight.
  4. Sand lightly, second coat, sand again. If you can feel the edge, it needs another pass.
  5. Prime the patch before painting or the spot will flash through forever.
Build the Little Builder birdhouse
  1. Lay out all pre-cut pieces from the kit and match them to the diagram.
  2. Glue and nail the walls to the floor panel — kids hold, adults drive the nails.
  3. Attach the roof panels with a slight overhang so rain runs off.
  4. Sand every edge smooth. Splinters end the fun fast.
  5. Paint it your colors, let it cure 24 hours, then hang it 5+ feet up facing away from prevailing wind.
Paint a room with zero streaks
  1. Prep is the job: fill holes, sand, tape trim, and wipe walls with a damp rag.
  2. Cut in edges with a 2.5-inch angled brush before you ever touch the roller.
  3. Roll in a W pattern, then smooth it out — never let the roller run dry on the wall.
  4. Keep a wet edge: work top to bottom, one wall at a time, without breaks.
  5. Pull tape while the paint is still slightly tacky, at a 45-degree angle.
Sharpen a mower blade safely
  1. Disconnect the spark plug wire first. Every time. No exceptions.
  2. Tip the mower with the air filter UP so oil doesn't flood it.
  3. Remove the blade, clamp it in a vise, and file at the factory angle — about 10 passes per side.
  4. Balance it on a nail through the center hole. If one side drops, file that side more.
  5. Reinstall, reconnect the plug, and mow. A sharp blade cuts clean; a dull one shreds and browns the grass.
Paint a room or accent wall
  1. Prep is the job: fill holes, sand, tape trim, and wipe walls with a damp rag.
  2. Cut in edges with a 2.5-inch angled brush before touching the roller.
  3. Roll in a W pattern and keep a wet edge — one wall at a time, no breaks.
  4. Two thin coats beats one thick coat; let the first dry fully.
  5. Pull tape while the paint is slightly tacky, at a 45-degree angle.
Turn bookcases into custom built-ins
  1. Line up basic bookcases or stock cabinets along the wall and anchor them to studs.
  2. Add trim, baseboards, and crown to close the gaps top and bottom.
  3. Swap in personal hardware — knobs and pulls that match your style.
  4. Caulk every seam, then paint everything one color for the built-in look.
  5. Style the shelves — the paint job does half the work.
Upgrade cabinet hardware and fixtures
  1. Remove one old knob or pull and measure the hole spacing before buying.
  2. Buy all new hardware in one finish for a pulled-together look.
  3. Swap knobs and pulls with a drill — ten minutes per door, tops.
  4. Replace dated light fixtures and faucets: water off, swap, water on.
  5. Step back — the kitchen or bath looks five years newer.
Install a kitchen backsplash
  1. Turn off outlets, remove cover plates, and clean the wall.
  2. Dry-lay your tile pattern on the counter before any mortar goes up.
  3. Spread thinset in small sections and set tile with spacers.
  4. Let it cure overnight, then grout — wipe haze off before it dries.
  5. Caulk the bottom edge where tile meets counter and reinstall plates.
Build an outdoor bench with planters
  1. Cut 2x lumber for the bench frame and fence pickets for the slats.
  2. Build two planter boxes to double as the bench legs.
  3. Assemble the frame with exterior screws — pre-drill to avoid splits.
  4. Line planters with landscape fabric, add soil and plants.
  5. Stain or seal everything — outdoor wood without sealer rots fast.
Frame a builder-grade bathroom mirror
  1. Measure the mirror and cut wood trim to fit with 45-degree corners.
  2. Paint or stain the trim before it goes on the wall.
  3. Apply liquid nails to the back of the trim.
  4. Press the frame directly onto the mirror edges and tape it in place.
  5. Let it cure 24 hours before removing the tape — instant custom mirror.

Kids' craft guides — free step-by-steps

Twelve family-tested crafts. Grab the supplies, make a mess, make a memory.

Painted Kindness Rocks
  1. Collect smooth outdoor stones and wash them clean; let them dry fully.
  2. Paint a base coat with acrylic paint and let it dry.
  3. Add positive words or bright patterns with paint pens or small brushes.
  4. Seal with a clear outdoor sealer so the art survives the weather.
  5. Hide them around the neighborhood for someone else to find.
Tissue Paper Suncatchers
  1. Cut a frame shape from cardstock or craft foam.
  2. Cover one side of the frame with clear contact paper, sticky side up.
  3. Arrange torn tissue paper shapes on the sticky surface.
  4. Seal with a second sheet of contact paper and trim the edges.
  5. Tape it to a sunny window and watch the colors glow.
Salt Dough Handprint Keepsakes
  1. Mix 1 cup flour, 1/2 cup salt, and 1/2 cup water into a dough.
  2. Roll it flat, about half an inch thick.
  3. Press the child's hand firmly into the dough.
  4. Bake at 200°F for 2–3 hours until hard.
  5. Paint, date the back, and keep it forever.
Paper Plate Sunflowers & Animals
  1. Paint the center of a plain paper plate brown.
  2. Cut petals from colored paper and glue them around the edge.
  3. Add googly eyes and drawn faces for animals, or seeds for sunflowers.
  4. Let everything dry flat.
  5. Hang them up — instant gallery wall.
Nature Collage & Leaf Paintbrushes
  1. Gather twigs, autumn leaves, and pine needles on a walk.
  2. Tie leaves and needles to twig handles with twine or rubber bands to make natural brushes.
  3. Dip them in paint and stamp patterns onto paper.
  4. Glue the best leaves into a nature collage.
  5. Label what you found — a science lesson hiding in art.
DIY Slime / Cloud Dough
  1. For slime: mix PVA glue with a spoonful of baking soda, then add contact solution a little at a time until it pulls together.
  2. For cloud dough: mix 2 cups cornstarch with 1/4 cup hair conditioner until crumbly-soft.
  3. Knead for a few minutes until the texture feels right.
  4. Add food coloring or glitter if you want the fancy version.
  5. Store in a sealed container — it keeps for weeks.
Handprint Canvas Art
  1. Start with a blank canvas or heavy art paper.
  2. Paint each family member's hand a different color.
  3. Press handprints onto the canvas to form animals, a family tree, or abstract art.
  4. Add details with brushes once the prints dry.
  5. Date and sign it — it only gets more valuable.
Fairy Gardens / Mini Terrariums
  1. Pick a shallow container — a bowl, tray, or old baking dish works.
  2. Layer pebbles for drainage, then potting soil.
  3. Plant small succulents or moss.
  4. Add miniature figurines, tiny houses, or painted stones.
  5. Mist lightly and set it where the family will see it daily.
Homemade Cornhole / Ring Toss
  1. Build a simple toss board from plywood or thick cardboard.
  2. Cut the hole (cornhole) or set up pegs (ring toss).
  3. Let every family member paint and decorate their own side.
  4. Make bean bags from old socks filled with rice, sewn shut.
  5. Keep score across the week — loser does dishes.
Birdhouses / Cereal-Box Bird Feeders
  1. For boxes: rinse a clean carton, cut feeding windows, and punch a hanging hole.
  2. For wood kits: assemble per the instructions — kids hold, adults drive nails.
  3. Decorate with outdoor-safe paint.
  4. Fill with seed and hang from a branch or hook.
  5. Keep a list of the birds that visit — backyard science.
DIY Slime / Modeling Clay
  1. For clay: mix 1 cup cornstarch, 1/2 cup baking soda, and 3/4 cup water in a pan.
  2. Heat on low, stirring, until it forms a dough ball.
  3. Cool, then knead smooth.
  4. Sculpt and shape together — air-dry finished pieces overnight.
  5. Paint the dried pieces the next day.
Family Time Capsule
  1. Decorate a large container or sturdy box together.
  2. Each person adds notes, small drawings, photos, and a list of current favorites.
  3. Add a newspaper front page or printed headlines from today.
  4. Seal it shut and write the open date on top — one year, five years, your call.
  5. Store it somewhere safe and try not to peek.
